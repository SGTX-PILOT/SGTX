    83→11.1 ADD‑ON 1 — GRAPH NEURAL NETWORK (GNN) RISK ENGINE & INSTITUTIONAL
    84→TRADE GRAPH
    85→
    86→11.1.1 Purpose
    87→
    88→The Graph Neural Network (GNN) Risk Engine serves two complementary
    89→purposes:
    90→
    91→1. Sanctions & Adversarial Risk Detection — Detects indirect sanctions
    92→links, hidden money‑laundering patterns, and adversarial relationships
    93→(e.g., a seller's Ultimate Beneficial Owner within 2 hops of a
    94→sanctioned entity).
    95→
    96→2. Institutional Trade Graph (Trust‑Based Mapping) — Maps positive,
    97→trust‑based relationships (trade frequency, financing relationships,
    98→co‑compliance, supply chain dependencies) to strengthen credit
    99→assessments, financing decisions, and network trust scores.
   100→
   101→All outputs are advisory or constraining (A2/A4) — they never recommend
   102→counterparties or autonomous actions.
   103→
   104→11.1.2 Architecture & Integration
   105→
   106→The GNN service is implemented as a Rust + PyTorch
   107→Geometric microservice (or a Python sidecar with a Rust gRPC wrapper).
   108→It exposes two main endpoints:
   109→
   110→-   /v1/gnn/risk — for sanctions and adversarial risk (called by
   111→    Governor on trade.initiate and financing.request).
   112→
   113→-   /v1/gnn/trust — for institutional trust graph queries (called on
   114→    demand by Financier Portal, Trust Passport, and Portfolio views).
   115→
   116→Model Architecture:
   117→
   118→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   119→  Component       Technology                  Purpose
   120→  --------------- --------------------------- ---------------------------
   121→  Graph           PyTorch Geometric           Learns node embeddings from
   122→  Convolutional                               corporate ownership, trade,
   123→  Network (GCN)                               and compliance graphs
   124→
   125→  Edge types      Ownership, directorship,    Detects proximity to
   126→  (adversarial)   sanctions list membership   sanctioned entities
   127→
   128→  Edge types      Trade frequency, financing  Builds positive trust graph
   129→  (trust)         agreements, shared          
   130→                  auditors, supply chain      
   131→                  links                       
   132→
   133→  Graph attention GATv2                       Weighs importance of
   134→                                              different relationship
   135→                                              types
   136→
   137→  Training        Weekly (off‑peak)           Using aggregated,
   138→  frequency                                   anonymised data across
   139→                                              sovereign nodes (or
   140→                                              federated learning)
   141→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   142→
   143→11.1.3 Sanctions & Adversarial Risk Output
   144→
   145→When called by the Governor, the GNN returns:
   146→
   147→json
   148→
   149→{
   150→
   151→"sanctions_proximity": 2,
   152→
   153→"graph_risk_score": 85,
   154→
   155→"explanation": "Seller's UBO (Person X) is 2 hops from sanctioned entity
   156→Y via Company Z.",
   157→
   158→"model_version": "v2.1",
   159→
   160→"inference_time_ms": 120
   161→
   162→}
   163→
   164→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   165→  Field                 Description
   166→  --------------------- ---------------------------------------------------------
   167→  sanctions_proximity   Minimum number of hops between any party (buyer, seller,
   168→                        logistics provider, financier) and a sanctioned entity
   169→
   170→  graph_risk_score      0–100 composite of adversarial risk (money laundering,
   171→                        sanctions evasion, hidden ownership)
   172→
   173→  explanation           Groq‑generated (A1) plain‑language summary
   174→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   175→
   176→Governor Integration: If sanctions_proximity ≤ 2 OR graph_risk_score ≥
   177→90, the Governor returns CONDITIONAL with a Decision Panel
   178→explanation: "This trade involves a party whose UBO is within 2 hops of
   179→a sanctioned entity. Enhanced due diligence required." The decision
   180→never suggests alternative counterparties.

   182→11.1.4 Institutional Trade Graph (Trust‑Based Mapping)
   183→
   184→Purpose: Map positive relationships while preserving privacy using ZK
   185→proofs. Used by:
   186→
   187→-   Financier Portal (credit assessment, portfolio risk)
   188→
   189→-   Trust Passport (network trust score)
   190→
   191→-   Dispute evidence (indirect relationship patterns)
   192→
   193→Edge Types & Weights:
   194→
   195→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   196→  Edge Type                Weight   Privacy Method        Data Source
   197→  ------------------------ -------- --------------------- -----------------------------
   198→  Trade frequency (last 12 1.0      ZK proof (count only, shipments
   199→  months)                           no identities)        
   200→
   201→  Financing relationship   1.5      ZK proof (existence,  financing_agreements
   202→                                    no amount)            
   203→
   204→  Co‑compliance (shared    0.5      Explicit consent      tenant_verified_ids
   205→  auditor / certifier)              required              
   206→
   207→  Supply chain dependency  0.8      ZK proof (indirect,   trade_requests (anonymised)
   208→                                    anonymised)           
   209→
   210→  Shared logistics         0.3      Aggregated, no        service_quotations
   211→  provider (LSP/SHIP)               consent needed        
   212→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   213→
   214→Output for Participant (Ego Network Only):
   215→
   216→json
   217→
   218→{
   219→
   220→"direct_connections": 47,
   221→
   222→"trusted_connections": 12,
   223→
   224→"network_trust_score": 82,
   225→
   226→"indirect_exposure": "2 hops to 3 financial institutions",
   227→
   228→"privacy_notice": "All counts are anonymised; no counterparty identities
   229→revealed."
   230→
   231→}

   233→Display Locations:
   234→
   235→-   Financier Portal — as an optional card in "Portfolio & Compliance"

   237→-   Trust Passport (Part 2.10) — as an optional dimension (opt‑in)

   239→-   Company Admin → Network — for traders (aggregated, no identifiers)

   366→11.2 ADD‑ON 2 — FEDERATED LEARNING NETWORK
   367→
   368→11.2.1 Purpose
   369→
   370→Train machine learning models across multiple sovereign nodes without
   371→exchanging raw trade data. Each node trains a local model on its own
   372→data (e.g., fraud detection, margin estimation, credit scoring), sends
   373→only encrypted model updates (gradients) to a secure aggregator, and
   374→receives a global model. This improves model accuracy for corridors with
   375→limited local data while preserving data sovereignty. The system never
   376→uses this to rank or recommend counterparties.
   377→
   378→11.2.2 Architecture & Integration
   379→
   380→  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   381→  Component      Technology                            Description
   382→  -------------- ------------------------------------- ----------------------
   383→  Coordinator    fed-learning-coordinator (Rust, Axum, Runs on a designated
   384→                 with OpenFL or Flower integration)    node (or one node per
   385→                                                       region). Orchestrates
   386→                                                       training rounds.
   387→
   388→  Participants   Each sovereign node runs              Triggers weekly
   389→                 a local-trainer sidecar               training on local
   390→                                                       data.
   391→
   392→  Privacy        Differential privacy (ε=0.5) applied  No raw data ever
   393→                 to each update; gradients encrypted   leaves the node.
   394→                 with secure aggregation protocol      
   395→                 (e.g., Shamir secret sharing or       
   396→                 homomorphic encryption)               
   397→
   398→  Aggregation    FedAvg (or a more robust algorithm)   Produces a global
   399→                                                       model, distributed to
   400→                                                       all nodes and
   401→                                                       hot‑loaded by the AI
   402→                                                       orchestrator.
   403→
   404→  Performance    Model drift is tracked; if accuracy   —
   405→  monitoring     drops below threshold, the node may   
   406→                 revert to the previous model.

   490→11.3 ADD‑ON 3 — CAUSAL INFERENCE ENGINE
   491→
   492→11.3.1 Purpose
   493→
   494→Identify root causes of disputes, delays, defaults, or quality failures
   495→— not just correlations. For example, "68% of the delay was due to a
   496→port strike, 22% due to carrier rerouting, 10% due to customs
   497→documentation." This provides actionable insights for insurers,
   498→arbitrators, and traders — but never suggests "blame the other party" or
   499→"switch provider". It is purely factual.
   500→
   501→11.3.2 Architecture & Integration
   502→
   503→  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   504→  Component     Description
   505→  ------------- ------------------------------------------------------------
   506→  Service       causal-engine (Python, using DoWhy + EconML, wrapped with a
   507→                Rust gRPC server for Governor integration)
   508→
   509→  Trigger       Asynchronously when a dispute is filed, or when a milestone
   510→                exceeds its SLA by >50% (advisory)
   511→
   512→  Input         Timeline of milestones (timestamps, delays), IoT sensor data
   513→  features      (temperature, shock, humidity), External events (weather,
   514→                port strikes from RIA, AIS delays), QC overrides, lab
   515→                results, Carrier and logistics provider performance scores
   516→
   517→  Methodology   DoWhy builds a causal graph (DAG) based on domain knowledge;
   518→                EconML estimates the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) and
   519→                contribution of each factor using double‑ML
   520→
   521→  Output        JSON with root causes and contribution percentages, stored
   522→                in causal_attribution. Groq generates a plain‑language
   523→                summary added to the evidence package
   524→  --------------------------------------------------------------------------

   679→Run weekly vulnerability scans against the SGTX staging environment (and
   680→optionally production with read‑only scans). Findings are automatically
   681→categorised, prioritised, and, if critical, block the CI/CD pipeline.
   682→This ensures that no vulnerability reaches production. The scans never
   683→target tenant data; they focus on platform APIs and infrastructure.

   759→11.6 ADD‑ON 6 — POST‑QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY (PQC)
   760→
   761→11.6.1 Purpose
   762→
   763→Protect long‑term records (contracts, Loom hash chains, identity
   764→credentials, audit logs) against future quantum computer attacks. While
   765→Ed25519 (used for daily signatures) is secure today, it is not
   766→quantum‑safe. The add‑on adds a second layer of Dilithium3 signatures
   767→(NIST standard) and optionally Kyber1024 for key exchange. This does not
   768→affect daily operations; the platform continues to use Ed25519 for
   769→performance, while archival stores also hold a Dilithium3 signature.
   770→
