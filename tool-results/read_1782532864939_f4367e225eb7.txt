    38→11.7 ADD‑ON 7 — EXPANDED ZERO‑KNOWLEDGE PROOFS (ZK) & PROOF OF RESERVES
    39→
    40→11.7.1 Purpose
    41→
    42→Zero‑Knowledge Proofs (ZK) enable privacy‑preserving verification
    43→without revealing underlying sensitive data. SGTX implements four core
    44→use cases, plus a phased proof‑of‑reserves mechanism:
    45→
    46→1. Financier proof‑of‑reserves — A financier proves they hold sufficient
    47→liquidity to back a financing offer without revealing wallet balance,
    48→address, or chain.
    49→
    50→2. Confidential trade terms — Buyer and seller agree on a price hidden
    51→from the platform; SGTX verifies only that the price is within legal and
    52→market bounds.
    53→
    54→3. Private settlement — A buyer proves they settled a payment without
    55→revealing the exact amount or PSP used.
    56→
    57→4. Proof of Reserves (phased) — Initially via Big Four attestation,
    58→later via real‑time ZK proofs, ensuring platform solvency and backing of
    59→obligations.
    60→
    61→All ZK proofs are optional and client‑side generated (WASM). The
    62→platform never forces their use.
    63→

    82→
    83→Use Case 1 — Financier Proof‑of‑Reserves:
    84→
    85→Before submitting a bid, a financier (Bank or PFI) can optionally
    86→generate a ZK proof that their total stablecoin or fiat reserves exceed
    87→the offered amount.
    88→
    89→Workflow:
    90→
    91→1.  Financier connects their wallet(s) or bank account(s) via read‑only
    92→    API (or manually uploads a Merkle tree of balances).
    93→
    94→2.  Client‑side script (or Financier Portal WASM module) generates a ZK
    95→    proof that the sum of selected reserves ≥ amount_offered.
    96→
    97→3.  Proof is sent to SGTX along with the bid.
    98→
    99→4.  Governor verifies the proof; if valid, the bid proceeds (subject to
   100→    other checks).
   101→
   102→5.  No wallet address, exact balance, or chain is revealed.
   103→
   104→Use Case 2 — Confidential Trade Terms:
   105→
   106→Seller can hide the EXW price and logistics breakdown from the platform;
   107→buyer sees only the final quoted price (including SGTX fee).
   108→
   109→Workflow:
   110→
   111→1.  Seller toggles "Make price confidential" in Quote Submission.
   112→
   113→2.  System computes a ZK proof that (EXW + logistics + SGTX fee + broker
   114→    fees) is within a plausible range (e.g., not zero, not exceeding
   115→    market average by >300%).
   116→
   117→3.  The proof is stored in seller_quotes.price_zk_proof; the raw price
   118→    components are not stored.
   119→
   120→4.  Buyer sees only the final total price.
   121→
   122→5.  During dispute resolution, the proof can be revealed to an
   123→    arbitrator if needed.
   124→
   125→Use Case 3 — Private Settlement:
   126→
   127→After settlement, the payer can request a ZK proof of payment without
   128→revealing the exact amount or PSP used.
   129→
   130→Workflow:
   131→
   132→1.  Buyer clicks "Request Private Settlement Proof" in the Settlement
   133→    tab.
   134→
   135→2.  Client‑side script generates a proof from the PSP receipt and
   136→    settlement instruction, proving that a transfer of at least the
   137→    contractual amount occurred to the correct beneficiary.
   138→
   139→3.  Proof is stored in settlement_confirmations.zk_proof.
   140→
   141→4.  The buyer can share the proof with auditors or regulators; they
   142→    verify it without seeing sensitive details.
   143→

   587→12A.1.0 Purpose & Design Philosophy
   588→
   589→The Smart Inbox is the default landing page for every authenticated user
   590→across all portals. It shows a prioritised action feed that tells the
   591→user exactly what to do next, with a Recommended Actions Widget at the
   592→top for one‑click execution of the single most important task.
   593→
   594→Core principles:
   595→
   596→-   Action‑first, never information‑first — users see only what requires
   597→    immediate attention.
   598→
   599→-   Trade‑centric — items link to USTNs, financing agreements, or
   600→    distressed lots.
   601→
   602→-   Role‑adaptive — items are derived from the user's permissions,
   603→    trader mode (and active dual‑mode context: Buyer or Seller), and
   604→    open obligations.
   605→
   606→-   Prioritised — urgency score computed deterministically (A4) using
   607→    rules that incorporate deadlines, SLAs, and risk signals. AI
   608→    (Groq/Ollama) generates the title and description after the score is
   609→    known.
   610→
   611→-   Dismissable & snoozable — users can snooze (2h, 4h, 8h, 24h) or
   612→    permanently dismiss items; actions sync across devices via NATS.
   613→
   614→-   Non‑marketplace — never suggests unknown counterparties or
   615→    unsolicited trades.
   616→
   617→-   Multi‑shipment aware — items for multi‑shipment contracts appear per
   618→    shipment when that shipment is pending action.
   619→
   620→-   Financing aware — financiers see bids pending, margin calls,
   621→    repayment reminders, and co‑financing opportunities.
   622→
   623→-   Distressed aware — sellers see outreach responses, buyer offers;
   624→    buyers see distressed listings from saved contacts.
   625→
   626→-   Dual‑mode aware — when the toggle is set to Buyer, the inbox shows
   627→    only buyer‑centric items; switching to Seller immediately refreshes
   628→    and shows seller‑centric items.

   676→12A.1.1.2 Urgency Scoring Model (Deterministic, A4)
