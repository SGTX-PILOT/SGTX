    72→11.6 ADD‑ON 6 — POST‑QUANTUM CRYPTOGRAPHY (PQC)
    73→
    74→11.6.1 Purpose
    75→
    76→Protect long‑term records (contracts, Loom hash chains, identity
    77→credentials, audit logs) against future quantum computer attacks. While
    78→Ed25519 (used for daily signatures) is secure today, it is not
    79→quantum‑safe. The add‑on adds a second layer of Dilithium3 signatures
    80→(NIST standard) and optionally Kyber1024 for key exchange. This does not
    81→affect daily operations; the platform continues to use Ed25519 for
    82→performance, while archival stores also hold a Dilithium3 signature.
    83→
    84→11.6.2 Architecture & Integration
    85→
    86→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    87→  Component      Description
    88→  -------------- ----------------------------------------------------------------
    89→  Two‑layer      For every new contract or Governor decision, the system
    90→  signature      generates both Ed25519 (fast) and Dilithium3 (quantum‑safe)
    91→                 signatures
    92→
    93→  Storage        Both signatures stored
    94→                 in governor_decisions.pqc_signatureand contracts.pqc_signature
    95→
    96→  Background     Cron job (pqc-resigner) runs weekly, re‑signing any record older
    97→  re‑signing     than 1 year that lacks a PQC signature. Uses offline HSM
    98→                 (software‑based SoftHSM) to access Dilithium3 private key
    99→
   100→  Verification   Endpoints /v1/verify/pqc allow external parties to verify the
   101→                 Dilithium3 signature using SGTX public key (published on status
   102→                 page)
   103→
   104→  Key generation Platform Governance Authority (multisig) generates Dilithium3
   105→                 key pair using dedicated offline machine. Private key split
   106→                 using Shamir Secret Sharing (3‑of‑5) and stored in HSMs. Public
   107→                 key published
   108→  -------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   133→11.7 ADD‑ON 7 — EXPANDED ZERO‑KNOWLEDGE PROOFS (ZK) & PROOF OF RESERVES
   134→
   135→11.7.1 Purpose
   136→
   137→Zero‑Knowledge Proofs (ZK) enable privacy‑preserving verification
   138→without revealing underlying sensitive data. SGTX implements four core
   139→use cases, plus a phased proof‑of‑reserves mechanism:
   140→
   141→1. Financier proof‑of‑reserves — A financier proves they hold sufficient
   142→liquidity to back a financing offer without revealing wallet balance,
   143→address, or chain.
   144→
   145→2. Confidential trade terms — Buyer and seller agree on a price hidden
   146→from the platform; SGTX verifies only that the price is within legal and
   147→market bounds.
   148→
   149→3. Private settlement — A buyer proves they settled a payment without
   150→revealing the exact amount or PSP used.
   151→
   152→4. Proof of Reserves (phased) — Initially via Big Four attestation,
   153→later via real‑time ZK proofs, ensuring platform solvency and backing of
   154→obligations.
   155→
   156→All ZK proofs are optional and client‑side generated (WASM). The
   157→platform never forces their use.
   158→
   159→11.7.2 Architecture & Integration
   160→
   161→Technology Stack:
   162→
   163→  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   164→  Component      Technology               Description
   165→  -------------- ------------------------ -----------------------------------
   166→  Plonky3        Rust, MIT                Recursive SNARK with fast
   167→                                          client‑side proving (WASM) and
   168→                                          lightweight server verification
   169→
   170→  Circuit        One‑time per use case    Done during add‑on activation
   171→  compilation                             
   172→
   173→  Verification   Server‑side (Governor or Sub‑millisecond latency
   174→                 dedicated verification   
   175→                 service)                 
   176→  ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
   177→
   178→Use Case 1 — Financier Proof‑of‑Reserves:
   179→
   180→Before submitting a bid, a financier (Bank or PFI) can optionally
   181→generate a ZK proof that their total stablecoin or fiat reserves exceed
   182→the offered amount.
   183→
   184→Workflow:
   185→
   186→1.  Financier connects their wallet(s) or bank account(s) via read‑only
   187→    API (or manually uploads a Merkle tree of balances).
   188→
   189→2.  Client‑side script (or Financier Portal WASM module) generates a ZK
   190→    proof that the sum of selected reserves ≥ amount_offered.
   191→
   192→3.  Proof is sent to SGTX along with the bid.
   193→
   194→4.  Governor verifies the proof; if valid, the bid proceeds (subject to
   195→    other checks).
   196→
   197→5.  No wallet address, exact balance, or chain is revealed.
   198→
   199→Use Case 2 — Confidential Trade Terms:
   200→
   201→Seller can hide the EXW price and logistics breakdown from the platform;
   202→buyer sees only the final quoted price (including SGTX fee).
   203→
   204→Workflow:
   205→
   206→1.  Seller toggles "Make price confidential" in Quote Submission.
   207→
   208→2.  System computes a ZK proof that (EXW + logistics + SGTX fee + broker
   209→    fees) is within a plausible range (e.g., not zero, not exceeding
   210→    market average by >300%).
   211→
   212→3.  The proof is stored in seller_quotes.price_zk_proof; the raw price
   213→    components are not stored.
   214→
   215→4.  Buyer sees only the final total price.
   216→
   217→5.  During dispute resolution, the proof can be revealed to an
   218→    arbitrator if needed.
   219→
   220→Use Case 3 — Private Settlement:
   221→
   222→After settlement, the payer can request a ZK proof of payment without
   223→revealing the exact amount or PSP used.
   224→
   225→Workflow:
   226→
   227→1.  Buyer clicks "Request Private Settlement Proof" in the Settlement
   228→    tab.
   229→
   230→2.  Client‑side script generates a proof from the PSP receipt and
   231→    settlement instruction, proving that a transfer of at least the
   232→    contractual amount occurred to the correct beneficiary.
   233→
   234→3.  Proof is stored in settlement_confirmations.zk_proof.
   235→
   236→4.  The buyer can share the proof with auditors or regulators; they
   237→    verify it without seeing sensitive details.
   238→
   239→Use Case 4 — Proof of Reserves (Phased Approach):
   240→
   241→Phase 1 — Big Four Attestation (Initial, Months 1–12):
   242→
   243→  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   244→  Aspect        Description
   245→  ------------- --------------------------------------------------------------
   246→  Auditors      Rotating annually among PwC, Deloitte, EY, KPMG
   247→
   248→  Content       Total assets (by currency and custodian), total liabilities
   249→                (outstanding fees, unsettled trades, financing guarantees),
   250→                reserve ratio (assets / liabilities) — must be ≥110% per
   251→                constitutional rule, composition breakdown (USD, EUR, gold,
   252→                other)
   253→
   254→  Publication   Signed PDF available at /.well-known/reserves/attestation;
   255→                displayed in Government Portal → Platform Reserves and Admin
   256→                Portal → Platform Health
   257→
   258→  Frequency     Quarterly, within 30 days of quarter end
   259→  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   260→
   261→Phase 2 — Real‑time ZK Proofs (Mature, after >80% financing volume uses
   262→ZK‑capable wallets):
   263→
   264→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   265→  Aspect         Description
   266→  -------------- --------------------------------------------------------
   267→  Transition     Platform Governance Authority votes (3/5) when adoption
   268→  trigger        threshold met
   269→
   270→  Daily proof    Aggregated Merkle tree of all reserve accounts
   271→  generation     (custodial wallets, bank accounts); ZK proof that total
   272→                 assets ≥ 110% of total liabilities
   273→
   274→  Proof          Secured, air‑gapped HSM (not client‑side) signed by SGTX
   275→  generation     
   276→
   277→  Public         GET /v1/reserves/proof — returns latest ZK proof and
   278→  verification   metadata
   279→  endpoint       
   280→
   281→  Privacy        Proof reveals only reserve ratio and commitment to asset
   282→                 list; no individual account balances disclosed

   680→12A.1 Smart Inbox with Recommended Actions Widget
   681→
   682→12A.1.0 Purpose & Design Philosophy
   683→
   684→The Smart Inbox is the default landing page for every authenticated user
   685→across all portals. It shows a prioritised action feed that tells the
   686→user exactly what to do next, with a Recommended Actions Widget at the
   687→top for one‑click execution of the single most important task.
   688→
   689→Core principles:
   690→
   691→-   Action‑first, never information‑first — users see only what requires
   692→    immediate attention.
   693→
   694→-   Trade‑centric — items link to USTNs, financing agreements, or
   695→    distressed lots.
   696→
   697→-   Role‑adaptive — items are derived from the user's permissions,
   698→    trader mode (and active dual‑mode context: Buyer or Seller), and
   699→    open obligations.
   700→
   701→-   Prioritised — urgency score computed deterministically (A4) using
   702→    rules that incorporate deadlines, SLAs, and risk signals. AI
   703→    (Groq/Ollama) generates the title and description after the score is
   704→    known.
   705→
   706→-   Dismissable & snoozable — users can snooze (2h, 4h, 8h, 24h) or
   707→    permanently dismiss items; actions sync across devices via NATS.
   708→
   709→-   Non‑marketplace — never suggests unknown counterparties or
   710→    unsolicited trades.
   711→
   712→-   Multi‑shipment aware — items for multi‑shipment contracts appear per
   713→    shipment when that shipment is pending action.
   714→
   715→-   Financing aware — financiers see bids pending, margin calls,
   716→    repayment reminders, and co‑financing opportunities.
   717→
   718→-   Distressed aware — sellers see outreach responses, buyer offers;
   719→    buyers see distressed listings from saved contacts.
   720→
   721→-   Dual‑mode aware — when the toggle is set to Buyer, the inbox shows
   722→    only buyer‑centric items; switching to Seller immediately refreshes
   723→    and shows seller‑centric items.

   771→12A.1.1.2 Urgency Scoring Model (Deterministic, A4)
   772→
   773→The inbox-service computes a base score using rules and then applies
   774→role‑specific modifiers. Scores are recalculated whenever the underlying
   775→state changes (e.g., deadline approaches, new offer received). The
   776→scoring rules are hot‑reloadable via OPA policy snippets.
   777→
   778→Master Scoring Table (Baseline):
   779→
   780→  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
   781→  Score   Description
   782→  ------- ----------------------------------------------------------------
   783→  100     Action required by a hard deadline <2h, missing it would
   784→          irreversibly block the trade
   785→
   786→  95      Human approval (e.g., contract signature) required, deadline
   787→          <24h
   788→
   789→  90      Counteroffer from counterparty unanswered >24h
   790→
   791→  85      Critical document missing, vessel ETA <72h
   792→
   793→  80      Fee payment attempt failed after 3rd retry; manual intervention
   794→          needed
   795→
   796→  75      New quote received, validity <1 day
   797→
   798→  70      Carrier risk score <50
   799→
   800→  65      Cold‑chain temperature excursion unacknowledged >2h
   801→
   802→  60      Provider invoice discrepancy >5% unresolved >48h
   803→
   804→  50      Upcoming milestone reminder (e.g., loading window starts in 24h)
   805→
   806→  40      Financing: bidding window closing in <4h (for financier)
   807→
   808→  35      Financing: repayment due in 7 days (for borrower)
   809→
   810→  30      General trade activity (e.g., "Shipment departed")
   811→
   812→  20      Distressed: new offer received
   813→
   814→  10      Completed trade, closed dispute, financed repaid (informational)

   850→Snooze & Dismiss:
   851→
   852→-   Users can snooze an item for 2h, 4h, 8h, or 24h.
   853→
   854→-   The item disappears and reappears after the snooze period unless the
   855→    underlying urgency score increases by ≥10 points (in which case it
   856→    reappears immediately).
   857→
   858→-   Dismissed items move to history but are not deleted.
   859→
   860→Cross‑device sync: Snooze/dismiss actions publish a NATS
   861→event inbox.item.state_changed. All active client sessions receive the
   862→event and update local Zustand store immediately. Conflict resolution:
   863→last write wins; losing write logged in inbox_history with action_taken
   864→= 'OVERWRITTEN'.
