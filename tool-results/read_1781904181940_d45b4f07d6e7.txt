   446→15.0 Purpose & Design Philosophy
   447→
   448→This part defines the service level agreements (SLAs) that govern the
   449→SGTX platform's production environment. These commitments cover
   450→availability, latency, recovery time objectives (RTO), and recovery
   451→point objectives (RPO) for all critical services that directly affect a
   452→tenant's ability to initiate, track, settle, or manage trades, financing
   453→agreements, distressed cargo, or disputes. The SLAs are contractually
   454→binding under the Master Service Agreement and are monitored entirely
   455→by self‑hosted, zero‑cost open‑source tools (Prometheus, Grafana, Loki,
   456→Blackbox exporter, custom sla-monitor in Rust). No external monitoring
   457→service or paid APM is used.
   458→
   459→AI assistance (advisory only):
   460→
   461→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   462→  AI Level        Use Cases
   463→  --------------- -------------------------------------------------------
   464→  A1 (Groq →      Generates plain‑language SLA reports, post‑mortem
   465→  Ollama)         summaries, and predictive uptime forecasts
   466→
   467→  A2 (HF local →  Analyses historical outage patterns to predict future
   468→  Ollama)         risks (advisory, never blocks)
   469→  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
   470→
   471→Key principles (non‑marketplace, orchestration‑only):
   472→
   473→-   Transparency — All SLAs are published on a public status page
   474→    (https://status.sgtx.io) and accessible via API.
   475→
   476→-   Accountability — SLA credits are automatically calculated and
   477→    applied to tenant statements; no manual claim required.
   478→
   479→-   Zero‑cost monitoring — All probes run on the same bare‑metal
   480→    infrastructure; no cloud dependencies.
   481→
   482→-   Continuous improvement — AI‑driven post‑mortems and predictive
   483→    scaling reduce downtime over time.
   484→
   485→Scope: All SLAs apply to the production environment (minimum three
   486→sovereign nodes, active‑active). Development and sandbox environments
   487→have no SLA. For multi‑shipment contracts, each shipment's services are
   488→monitored independently; a delay in one shipment does not affect SLA for
   489→others. Conditional QC holds and deferred payment guarantees have their
   490→own SLAs defined in this part.

   610→15.1.3 Definitions
   611→
   612→  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
   613→  Term           Definition
   614→  -------------- -----------------------------------------------------------
   615→  Availability   (total_minutes_in_month — downtime_minutes) /
   616→                 total_minutes_in_month. Downtime is any period where the
   617→                 service responds with 5xx errors for >90% of requests, as
   618→                 measured by sla-monitor from at least two geographic
   619→                 locations (independent probes).
   620→
   621→  Latency (p95)  95th percentile of request latency, measured by Prometheus
   622→                 histograms.
   623→
   624→  RTO (Recovery  Maximum time from declaration of a disaster (automatically
   625→  Time           by AIOps or manually by Platform Governance Authority) to
   626→  Objective)     full restoration of the service in at least one region.
   627→
   628→  RPO (Recovery  Maximum acceptable data loss measured in time. For most
   629→  Point          services, zero due to synchronous replication
   630→  Objective)     (PostgreSQL synchronous_commit = on, NATS JetStream
   631→                 mirroring). For Tenant Message Generation, loss of a
   632→                 message is not critical (template fallback).
